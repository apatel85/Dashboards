How the numbers are built — and backtested

Expected yards. Each player's per-game rate is first regressed toward his position's average (blended = (games × rate + 2 × position avg) ÷ (games + 2)), then scaled by this week's matchup with the multiplier shrunk halfway back to neutral (mult′ = 1 + 0.5 × (opp allowed ÷ league avg − 1)). League baselines through Week 10: 112.0 rush yards and 218.5 pass yards allowed per game.

Comfortable max is that expected number itself — his average outcome in this game against this defense, rounded to whole yards. It is not a season ceiling or a best-case tail. P(60+) uses a normal approximation with σ = 0.65 × expected yards; "likely" means P ≥ 55%.

Touchdowns count physical scorers only — a QB's passing TDs never count; a QB who runs it in does, on rushing TDs alone. Josh Allen clears the bar again this week on 11 rushing TDs in 9 games — two of them 1-yard plunges at the Jets on a five-yard rushing day. TD rates get stronger regression than yardage rates (weight 6, held this week — see the backtest verdict in the Results tab), and a player needs at least 3 games of data to make the 1+ TD board. The model side runs P(1+) = 1 − e^(−λ), P(2+) = 1 − e^(−λ)(1 + λ); "likely 1+ TD" means P ≥ 50%. Every row shows its games sample — thin samples are the main reason the raw model used to overpromise. From this week's boards on, the probability published on both TD boards is the Vegas blend below, not this λ on its own.

The Vegas blend — live from Week 11. The Week 10 head-to-head is the reason: graded against the books' closing lines, Vegas beat the dashboard on Brier score (0.222 vs 0.238) and was closer on 18 of the 33 rows where a line existed — and the edge sat on the TD boards, where its anytime-TD prices ran higher than the model on all 8 comparable 1+ TD rows and were right on 6 of them. So the TD rate now blends 50/50 with the market: λ_vegas = −ln(1 − p_anytime) from the anytime-TD odds — vig-adjusted when both the Yes and No prices are posted, the raw implied probability when only the Yes is — then λ_blended = 0.5 × λ_model + 0.5 × λ_vegas, P(1+) = 1 − e^(−λ_blended) and P(2+) = 1 − e^(−λ_blended)(1 + λ_blended), except that a directly posted 2+ TD price replaces the Poisson P(2+) where the book offers one. The blended probability is the published probability. Worked example, from the Week 10 comparison: Jahmyr Gibbs — λ_model 0.98, anytime TD −245 (71.0%) → λ_vegas 1.24 → λ_blended 1.11 → P(1+) 67%, between the model's 63% and the market's 71% — and his posted 2+ price (+230, 30.3%) landed almost exactly on the blended Poisson figure (30.4%). Gibbs then scored twice in Munich. This week's rows run the same math live: Gibbs' Week 11 row shows model λ 0.97, market λ 1.19 (−230) and the blended 1.08 that prices him at 66%.

Yard boards take the line as a check, not an input. The model's expected yards and P(60+) stay the published numbers, but each row now carries a Vegas line column (blank where no line is posted) and a Vegas cooler flag when the line sits 10 or more yards below the model's expected yards. Week 10 was the flag's first live test and it went 2-for-2: Brock Bowers (line 57.5 vs expectation 73) managed 18 yards before his ankle ended his day, and Tetairoa McMillan (64.5 vs 77) managed 35 — while the rows where the market sat close ran true (James Cook: line 91.5, model 99, actual 157; Matthew Golden: line 68.5, model 71, actual 70). Three rows trip the flag this week — Walker, Higgins and Bowers — each for a stated reason on its row. Lines are entered at refresh time, in the two-pass Step 3 above, from public odds aggregators such as Win Daily, OddsChecker and Action Network. One honest limit: historical prop lines aren't recoverable in bulk, so the blend could not be backtested across Weeks 2–10 — Week 10 is the one week of evidence behind it, and this week's grading is its first live validation.
