How the numbers are built — and backtested

Expected yards. Each player's per-game rate is first regressed toward his position's average (blended = (games × rate + 2 × position avg) ÷ (games + 2)), then scaled by this week's matchup with the multiplier shrunk halfway back to neutral (mult′ = 1 + 0.5 × (opp allowed ÷ league avg − 1)). League baselines through Week 8: 112.8 rush yards and 219.2 pass yards allowed per game.

Comfortable max is that expected number itself — his average outcome in this game against this defense, rounded to whole yards. It is not a season ceiling or a best-case tail. P(60+) uses a normal approximation with σ = 0.65 × expected yards; "likely" means P ≥ 55%.

Touchdowns count physical scorers only — a QB's passing TDs never count; a QB who runs it in does, on rushing TDs alone. Josh Allen clears the bar again this week on 9 rushing TDs in 7 games — at Minnesota, against the league's lowest total-TD allowance. TD rates get stronger regression than yardage rates (weight 5, held from last week — see the backtest verdict in the Results tab), and a player needs at least 3 games of data to make the 1+ TD board. P(1+) = 1 − e^(−λ), P(2+) = 1 − e^(−λ)(1 + λ); "likely 1+ TD" means P ≥ 50%. Every row shows its games sample — thin samples are the main reason the raw model used to overpromise.
