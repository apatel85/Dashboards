How the numbers are built — and backtested

Expected yards. Each player's per-game rate is first regressed toward his position's average (blended = (games × rate + 2 × position avg) ÷ (games + 2)), then scaled by this week's matchup with the multiplier shrunk halfway back to neutral (mult′ = 1 + 0.5 × (opp allowed ÷ league avg − 1)). League baselines through Week 11: 112.0 rush yards and 218.5 net pass yards allowed per game.

Comfortable max is that expected number itself — his average outcome in this game against this defense, rounded to whole yards. It is not a season ceiling or a best-case tail. P(60+) uses a normal approximation with σ = 0.65 × expected yards; "likely" means P ≥ 55%.

Touchdowns count physical scorers only — a QB's passing TDs never count; a QB who runs it in does, on rushing TDs alone. Josh Allen sits exactly on the bar this week on 11 rushing TDs in 10 games — and Week 11 was the cautionary tale: zero rushing attempts against Miami, the first such game of his season. TD rates get stronger regression than yardage rates (weight 6, held this week — see the backtest verdict in the Results tab), and a player needs at least 3 games of data to make the 1+ TD board. The model side runs P(1+) = 1 − e^(−λ), P(2+) = 1 − e^(−λ)(1 + λ); "likely 1+ TD" means P ≥ 50%. Every row shows its games sample — thin samples are the main reason the raw model used to overpromise. On both TD boards, the probability published is the Vegas blend below where a market price exists — five of this week's eleven 1+ TD rows had no anytime price posted at build time and publish this λ on its own, labeled as such on their rows.

The Vegas blend — live from Week 11, validated in Week 11. The Week 10 head-to-head earned it: graded against the books' closing lines, Vegas beat the dashboard on Brier score (0.222 vs 0.238) and was closer on 18 of the 33 rows where a line existed. Its first live week then ran 7 of 9 against a 60% average predicted on the 1+ TD board — above price — with the two misses being an inactive (Walker) and a zero-rushing-attempt game (Allen), not pricing errors. The TD rate blends 50/50 with the market: λ_vegas = −ln(1 − p_anytime) from the anytime-TD odds — vig-adjusted when both the Yes and No prices are posted, the raw implied probability when only the Yes is — then λ_blended = 0.5 × λ_model + 0.5 × λ_vegas, P(1+) = 1 − e^(−λ_blended) and P(2+) = 1 − e^(−λ_blended)(1 + λ_blended), except that a directly posted 2+ TD price replaces the Poisson P(2+) where the book offers one. The blended probability is the published probability. Worked example, live this week: Jahmyr Gibbs — λ_model 1.12, anytime TD −275 (73.3%) → λ_vegas 1.32 → λ_blended 1.22 → P(1+) 71%, between the model's 67% and the market's 73%. One honest limit, unchanged: historical prop lines aren't recoverable in bulk, so the blend could not be backtested across Weeks 2–11 — Weeks 10–11 are the evidence behind it, and each Wednesday's grading adds a week.

Yard boards take the line as a check, not an input. The model's expected yards and P(60+) stay the published numbers, but each row now carries a Vegas line column (blank where no line is posted) and a Vegas cooler flag when the line sits 10 or more yards below the model's expected yards. The flag's live ledger: Week 10 went 2-for-2 (Bowers 18 yards, McMillan 35 — both under 60), and Week 11's three flagged rows all finished under 60 too — Higgins managed 43 against his 79.5 line, while Walker and Bowers never played at all. Read that honestly: the flag is 5-for-5 on outcomes, but two of the five are availability the market knew about, not yardage wisdom. Two rows trip the flag this week — Walker (65.5 vs 90, the groin and the short week) and Smith-Njigba (97.5 vs 113, the Darnold question) — each for a stated reason on its row. Lines are entered at refresh time, in the two-pass Step 3 above, from public odds aggregators such as Sharp Football, Statz, Win Daily and OddsChecker.
