## 1. Elimination Development & House-Training

### Bladder-hold planning ranges (calm conditions; awake/excited/puppies hold less)
| Age | Approximate max hold |
|---|---|
| 8 weeks | 1–2 hours |
| 12 weeks | 2–3 hours |
| 16 weeks | 3–4 hours |
| 20 weeks | 4–6 hours [4] |

- Two popular rules of thumb: **one hour per month of age** [5] and the AKC's **"age in months, plus one"** (e.g., a 3-month-old puppy → every 4 hours) [2]. **These are practical conventions, not validated physiological laws** [1][3] — the second variant is often misquoted, and neither has peer-reviewed validation.
- **Veterinary bottom line:** treat bladder-capacity formulas as planning ceilings, **not targets**; the goal is to take the puppy out *before* he needs to go, not to test the limit [3].
- **Overnight** sleep suppresses urgency, so puppies may sleep longer stretches than their daytime holds — but variation is large and it doesn't transfer to waking hours [4].
- **Reliable control:** most puppies gain real control around **4–6 months** (AKC) [5]; consistent house habits typically take 4–6 months [9].

### Dr. Ian Dunbar's errorless method (leading expert)
- **Prevention + immediate reward:** never give an untrained puppy a chance to make a mistake indoors (supervise, confine, schedule); reward elimination *immediately* at the correct spot — delayed rewards fail [7].
- **Structured progression:** start ~hourly potty breaks; stretch intervals by **1 hour per week** as successes accumulate; if an accident occurs, **revert to shorter intervals and continue** [7].
- The Humane Society framework mirrors this: management, routine, immediate reinforcement of outdoor elimination, zero punishment for accidents [8].
- **Timing triggers:** after waking, eating/drinking, play, and crate release — these are the highest-yield outing windows [9].

## 2. Feeding Fundamentals

### Resting vs. daily energy requirements
- **RER = 70 × (body weight in kg)^0.75** [10].
- **Standard DER multipliers for puppies** (Hemopet, clinical protocol): **×3.0 for puppies under 4 months; ×2.0 for puppies 4 months and older** [11].
- **Individual variation is large:** adjust ±50% based on body-condition score, weight trend, activity, stool quality, and vet input; the multiplier is a starting point, not a prescription [11].
- **Simba marker (Oct 3):** he has now crossed 4 months, so the Hemopet starting multiplier steps down from ×3.0 to ×2.0; the app's configurable 300–330 kcal/day target remains consistent with ~2× RER (≈351 kcal at 3.40 kg), adjusted from observed body condition.
- **Small-breed caveat:** standard equations (NRC 2006) can **overestimate** energy needs of small breeds by ~20% — the Waltham puppy equation (Dobenecker-based) tends to fit better for small dogs; always adjust from observed body condition [21][22].

### AAFCO nutritional-adequacy statements (what to look for on the label)
- Choose foods with an AAFCO statement for **"growth"** or **"all life stages"**; "all life stages" *includes* growth by definition [12].
- **"Large breed" growth formulas differ:** extra restrictions (calcium ≤1.8% DM, Ca:P ratio 1:1–1.8:1) apply only to dogs expected to exceed 70 lbs — **irrelevant for Simba** [14][15].
- Growth minimums: protein ~22.5% and fat ~8.5% (dry-matter basis, post-2016 AAFCO revision) [13].
- **Don't supplement:** never add calcium or vitamin D to an already complete growth diet — excess calcium impairs skeletal development in growing puppies [14].

### Meal frequency & diet transitions
| Age | Meals/day |
|---|---|
| 6–12 weeks | 4 |
| 3–6 months | 3 |
| 6+ months | 2 [16][17][18][19] |

- **Small-breed refinement:** 3–4 meals through 6 months (smaller meals protect against hypoglycemia and bloat risk) [20][16].
- Keep him on puppy/growth food until **12–18 months** (small breeds at the earlier end) [16].

## 3. Sleep Needs

| Age | Typical total sleep/day |
|---|---|
| Young puppy (8–12 wks) | 18–20 hours |
| 3–6 months (incl. Simba, ~18 wks) | 14–18 hours |
| Adult | 12–14 hours [23][24][25] |

- Sleep supports memory consolidation (Kis et al.), behavior regulation, growth, and immune function [26][23].
- **Don't enforce a rigid quota** — track the individual's baseline; sudden changes, inability to settle, or lethargy while awake are the real signals [23][24].
- Enforced naps in a crate/quiet spot help overstimulated puppies settle; expect ~3–4 naps/day in the first year [24].

## 4. Crate Training

- **Positive association only:** feed treats/meals inside; build closed-door time gradually in short successful steps; never use as punishment [27][28].
- **Phased duration:** begin with seconds/minutes awake, then work toward nap-length confinement and overnight [28][29].
- **Bathroom breaks before crating and immediately after release** [29].
- **Conservative daytime limits:** daytime confinement should be *more* conservative than overnight holds — **no formal AAHA/AVMA age-by-age crate-duration standard was found**; treat bladder-hold formulas as ceilings, never as permission to confine for the full theoretical max [1][3][28].
- Proper sizing: stand up, turn around, lie down comfortably [29].

## 5. Teething Timeline

| Age | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 3–4 weeks | Deciduous teeth start erupting |
| 6–8 weeks | Full 28 deciduous teeth |
| 12–16 weeks | Adult teeth begin (incisors first, then premolars, canines later) |
| 6–7 months | Full 42 permanent teeth [30][31][32] |

- **Small-breed caution:** retained baby teeth are "particularly common in small and toy-breed dogs" — if baby teeth persist alongside adult teeth at ~6 months, have the vet check; retained teeth cause misalignment and decay [37 in breed doc].
- **Simba marker:** at ~18 weeks he is in active adult-tooth eruption — expect heavy chewing, drooling, mild gum bleeding, possible appetite dips; offer appropriate chews [33].

## 6. Vaccination Windows & Safe Socialization

- **Core (DA2PP) series:** starts 6–8 weeks, repeated every 2–4 weeks **until at least 16 weeks**; booster at ~1 year, then every 3 years (2022 AAHA guidelines + 2024 update) [34][35].
- **Rabies:** from 12–16 weeks per local law and product label [34].
- **Leptospirosis is now AAHA-core** (re-designated ~2025 on ACVIM/WSAVA consensus — rising prevalence and zoonotic risk): 2-dose puppy series from 12 weeks, then annual boosters [53].
- **Lifestyle vaccines** (Bordetella, Lyme, flu): per local risk + vet advice [36][37].
- **Simba marker (Oct 3):** DA2PP series window (to 16 weeks) is closed — next milestone is the ~1-year booster. If his lepto 2-dose series started at 12 weeks it should also be complete; confirm against his vet records.
- **Why repeated doses:** maternal antibodies can neutralize early vaccines — the series exists to catch the window when they wane [36].
- **The socialization debate:** AVSAB consensus — **do not isolate the puppy until the series ends**; controlled socialization (clean, low-risk settings; known healthy dogs; carry in public) can start after initial vaccination/deworming. Avoid dog parks and unknown/unvaccinated dogs until the vet confirms protection [38][39][40].
- The classic evidence: Dr. R.K. Anderson's 2013 parvo study — early, careful socialization before 16 weeks showed **no increased parvo risk** vs. later socialization [39].

## 7. Safe Exercise for Growing Puppies

- **The "5 minutes per month of age" rule:** originated with the **UK Kennel Club** — it is a rough rule of thumb with **no published scientific validation** [41][42].
- **Evidence-based pushback:** trainers/vets note it ignores breed, size, surface, intensity, and individual fitness; the *type* of activity matters more than the minute count [43][44].
- **Practical guidance:** prefer puppy-led exploration, sniffing, free play on soft surfaces; avoid forced running, long marches, repetitive jumping, sharp repetitive turns, and repetitive ball-chasing (high concussive load) [43][44].
- **Watch the individual:** increase activity gradually; stiffness, reluctance to move, soreness, or behavior change after exercise = back off and consult the vet [43][44].

## 8. Purina Fecal Scoring Chart (1–7)

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **1** | Very hard, dry pellets — difficult to pass |
| **2** | Firm but pliable, segmented; little/no residue — **Purina's "ideal"** |
| **3** | Moist log, holds form; some residue left |
| **4** | Very moist/soggy log — loses shape when lifted |
| **5** | Very moist but distinct piles — loses form |
| **6** | Has texture but no defined shape — piles or spots |
| **7** | Watery, no texture — flat puddle [45][46][47] |

- **"Gold standard" framing:** many clinical sources accept **scores 2–3 as normal**; Purina itself identifies **2** as ideal [47].
- For the app: trend deviations from the puppy's personal baseline matter more than any single score.

## 9. GI Distress — When to Contact the Vet

**Urgent** — call the vet / emergency clinic for: blood in vomit or stool (including black/tarry stool); repeated vomiting or inability to keep water down; frequent watery diarrhea; marked lethargy/weakness/collapse; pale gums; painful/swollen abdomen; **retching with a distended abdomen**; suspected toxin or foreign-body ingestion; **vomiting/diarrhea in an incompletely vaccinated puppy** (parvo risk) [48][49][51].
- **Puppies warrant earlier contact than adults** — dehydration and hypoglycemia develop faster [49].

**Monitor at home:** single mild vomiting/diarrhea episodes in an otherwise bright, hydrated puppy can often be watched briefly with bland diet and water access [51].

**Dehydration indicators:** tacky/dry gums, sunken eyes, reduced urination, weakness, abnormal skin elasticity — the skin-tent test is a rough field check, not definitive [52].

**Vomit-color guidance (PDSA):** yellow/green = bile (empty stomach); dark red/brown/black or coffee-ground texture = possible blood → urgent; foamy white can signal bloat (especially with a swollen abdomen) → emergency [50].

## 10. Where Experts Disagree (Summary)
1. **Bladder-hold rules:** "one hour per month of age" vs. "months + one" (AKC) — both are conventions, not physiology; neither is peer-reviewed.
2. **Exercise:** the 5-min/month rule of thumb vs. evidence-based critics who prioritize activity *type* and individual response over minutes.
3. **Diet labels:** "all life stages" is *adequate* for puppies but not *optimized* vs. puppy-specific growth formulas — split among nutritionists.
4. **Socialization:** early controlled exposure (AVSAB) vs. strict isolation until the vaccine series ends — the profession's consensus favors controlled exposure.
5. **AAFCO numbers:** older references (22% protein / 8% fat) vs. post-2016 AAFCO revision (22.5% / 8.5%) — check publication dates when comparing label claims.
