Finance: the strategies in your saves

Thirteen distinct approaches show up across the Finance & Trading saves — from rules-based systems you could test in TradeDesk to sales funnels dressed up as strategies. Each one names the video it came from.

01Trend-filter pullback: 200-day average + RSI dip

Only buy when price is above its 200-day moving average (trend is up). Enter when the 10-day RSI drops below 30 (a short-term dip inside the uptrend). Exit when RSI crosses back above 40, or after 10 days — whichever comes first. The video backtested Rayner Teo's rules on Indian stocks and reports 34.8% in a year, a 78% win rate, and an 11.6% worst drawdown — those are the creator's backtest numbers, not independently verified.

From: Rayner Teo strategy, algo-checked — Sahil Sawhney

02Watch the 50-day rate of change, not the price

Hedge-fund trader Jordi Visser tracks one number: how much a stock or index has moved over the last 50 days. If you have held something 50 days, the news has been good, and it still has not moved, its rate of change has rolled to zero — frustrated holders are about to sell. The signal is the change in momentum (the 'second derivative'), not the price level itself.

From: $4B Hedge Fund Trader — Titans Of Tomorrow

03Trade around VWAP — the institutions' average price

VWAP (volume-weighted average price) is the average price weighted by how much actually traded at each level — the line big funds use to judge whether they got a fair price. The masterclass sets it up on a 1-minute chart and treats moves away from and back to VWAP as the day's roadmap: above it, buyers are in control; below it, sellers are.

From: VWAP Masterclass — RdY Investments (published Nov 2025, the oldest video in this set)

04Read order flow and options positioning (GEX), not just candles

Two saves make the same point from different angles: one trader's first payout came after switching to gamma exposure (GEX) levels, order flow, and options data; another lists three study resources on footprint charts, liquidity, and absorption — how to see where big orders are actually being filled instead of guessing from candle shapes.

From: The Strategy That Finally Paid Me Out — Leah Farnsworth · Order flow, footprint & liquidity resources — TC Trades Tyler

05ICT / Smart Money concepts for NQ and ES futures

A curated watchlist of three free YouTube videos on ICT (Inner Circle Trader) concepts — liquidity, market structure, and institutional order flow — aimed at Nasdaq (NQ) and S&P (ES) futures day traders. This is a study plan, not a system: the value is in the three linked lessons, in order.

From: 3 videos that could completely change how you day trade — TC Trades Tyler

06Don't forecast — structure the trade so you don't have to

Two saves from the same account argue the institutional options mindset: big desks survive not by predicting direction but by structuring positions (spreads, defined risk, premium) that profit without needing the forecast to be right. The amateur asks 'where is it going?'; the desk asks 'what am I paid if I'm wrong?'

From: Every Options Trade Has Two Sides (two saves) — Market Moves

07Sell options weekly, but only from a pre-vetted watchlist

A rules-based premium-selling routine: keep a pre-screened list of stocks you are willing to trade, let a tool scan that list each week, and take only the entries and exits it flags. The discipline being sold is the short list and the fixed routine — less chart time, same trades every week.

From: The 1 boring trading tool I use to sell options every week — Market Moves

08Screen for oversold stocks on a schedule

A dated screen (Nov 9, 2026) that hunts for oversold names — stocks beaten down far enough that a bounce becomes the higher-probability trade. Useful as a watchlist generator; the save itself is just the screen output, so the method behind the screen is not documented in the caption.

AI repos: which ones are actually worth your time

Dozens of repos are name-dropped across your saves. Most links are gated behind “comment a keyword” on the Reel itself. These are the ones that check out, ranked by usefulness for how you work — building apps with AI and researching trades.

1Start here: Karpathy Skills — one file that makes your coding agent careful

A single instruction file for Claude Code, built from Andrej Karpathy's public observations on how AI coding assistants go wrong — by multica-ai, not by Karpathy himself. Four rules: think before coding, keep it simple, change only what is needed, and verify the work before calling it done. The Reel cites 220K+ stars; independent write-ups in November 2026 put it at roughly 204K–229K stars, among the most-starred repos on GitHub. It costs nothing, takes two minutes to add to a project, carries no security surface, and improves every app you build with an agent — TradeDesk, WagWise, Quixera work included. Highest value per minute of anything in your saves.

Repo: github.com/multica-ai/andrej-karpathy-skills · From the Reel by 100xEngineers (published Nov 7, 2026)

2For your trading research: TradingAgents — a whole AI trading firm, open source

The repo behind two of your saves (the TradingAgents Reel and, in spirit, the four 'Trading Research Team in Claude Code' saves). Four analyst agents (fundamentals, news, sentiment, technicals) write reports, bull and bear agents debate, a trader proposes, a risk team checks, and a portfolio manager approves — with a decision log that later grades its own calls against real returns. From Tauric Research (UCLA/MIT authors), with a published paper; a November 2026 write-up counted ~115K stars. It places no orders and its own README says research only, not financial advice — which makes it a safe way to test whether multi-agent debate improves your TradeDesk research before you build anything similar yourself. Runs on an LLM API key or locally with Ollama, on US and Indian tickers. Limitation (Nov 24 audit): the Reel describes the original 2024 LangGraph framework, but the current repo is a rebuilt terminal agent needing a Tauric API key (or other LLM key) for data, and the bull/bear debate structure is no longer in the current README.

Repo: github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents · Paper: arXiv 2412.20138

3The reusable building block: Browser Use

Headliner of the '7 GitHub Repos to Build AI Agents' Reel: it lets an agent drive a real browser — open pages, click, fill forms — the way a person would. ~122K stars on recent counts. Of the seven repos in that video (agent memory, scientific skills, diagram design, community cybersecurity skills — not affiliated with Anthropic despite the name — harness-engineering patterns, OpenViking context), Browser Use is the one with the widest reuse across your projects: QA capture, web research, and any workflow that today needs you clicking through a site.

Repo: github.com/browser-use/browser-use · Named in the Reel by Coding Simplified Space (published Nov 11, 2026) — full repo links for all seven are gated behind commenting on that Reel.
