    Foods & Items to AVOID - Toxic to Dogs
    CategoryDangerous Items
    Extremely toxicChocolate (darker = worse), grapes & raisins (kidney failure even small amounts), xylitol (sugar-free gum/candy/toothpaste), macadamia nuts, onions, garlic, alcohol
    Toxic in quantityAvocado, caffeine, fatty foods (pancreatitis risk), salt, raw bread dough/yeast (bloat + alcohol poisoning)
    MedicationsHuman pain relievers (ibuprofen, acetaminophen, aspirin, naproxen), antidepressants - never give human medication without vet direction
    Household/plantsBleach, ammonia, antifreeze, rodenticides, lilies, sago palm, azaleas, tulips, oleander
    Poisoning Symptoms to Watch For
    SymptomWhat It May Indicate
    Vomiting or diarrheaMost common and often first sign of poisoning
    Excessive droolingReaction to something harmful ingested
    Lethargy or weaknessGeneral systemic reaction
    Tremors or seizuresNeurological toxicity - urgent
    Difficulty breathingRespiratory involvement - urgent
    Pale gums, rapid breathingPossible anemia (onion/garlic toxicity)
    Distended/swollen stomachPossible bread dough ingestion or bloat - urgent
    Increased thirst/urinationPossible kidney involvement (grapes/raisins)
    What To Do Right Away If Poisoning Is Suspected
    • Stay calm - panicking wastes critical time.
    • Remove your puppy from the toxin source and take away any remaining substance/packaging.
    • Do NOT induce vomiting unless a vet or poison control specifically tells you to - some substances cause more harm coming back up.
    • Call immediately: ASPCA Animal Poison Control (888) 426-4435 or Pet Poison Helpline (855) 764-7661, or your vet.
    • Have the product packaging or a sample ready to describe exactly what was ingested and how much.
    • Get to the vet or nearest emergency animal clinic immediately if advised - time is critical for a good outcome.
    Save both poison control numbers in your phone now, before an emergency happens.

    Dental Care: Brushing Frequency & Schedule by Age
    Age/StageTeeth BrushingCoat BrushingBathing
    8-12 weeks (now)Taste/touch introduction only - no full brushing yet, gums sore from teethingLight daily brushingGentle/quick only if needed
    3-4 monthsBegin 2-3x/week once teething calms3x/week minimumEvery 3-4 weeks
    4-6 months3-4x/week, building toward daily3-4x/week as adult coat comes inEvery 3-4 weeks
    6+ months (adult routine)Daily (or as close as consistently possible)Daily light brushing, thorough 2-3x/weekEvery 3-8 weeks depending on activity/odor
    Daily brushing is the gold standard, but 3-4x/week still delivers real dental benefit. Bathing interval loosens as they mature (over-bathing strips protective skin oils), while coat brushing frequency stays high throughout since Cavapoo hair never stops needing maintenance.
    How to Brush Puppy Teeth - Step by Step
    • Use dog-safe enzymatic toothpaste + soft pet toothbrush or finger brush. Never use human toothpaste (contains xylitol/fluoride, toxic to dogs).
    • Let them taste it first - dab on your finger, let them lick it, before any brushing attempt.
    • Keep it short at first: 10-20 seconds total for a young puppy, gradually building to 1-2 minutes.
    • Lift the lip gently, brush in small circular motions on the outer tooth surfaces (where most plaque forms).
    • Front teeth first, then work back toward the molars.
    • End on a positive note - treat and praise every time, regardless of how much you got done.

    Potty & Pee Pad Training
    • Pick one fixed spot away from food/water/sleep area.
    • Let puppy sniff and explore the pad.
    • Take to pad hourly plus after wake/eat/drink/play.
    • Watch for sniffing/circling signs, act fast.
    • Reward instantly when they finish.
    • Never punish accidents; clean with enzyme cleaner.
    • Optionally move pad toward door then outside over time.
